Adaptive Retrieval for
Evidence-Grounded Answers

Research Methods Group · 2026
研究示例材料，非真实发表论文

Abstract

Retrieval-augmented generation can support verifiable answers. Its value depends on the relevance of the evidence and on how faithfully the answer preserves the limits of that evidence.

3. Evidence relevance and answer reliability

We examine when retrieved evidence helps a reader distinguish supported claims from fluent but unsupported statements.

Retrieval improves factual accuracy when the evidence corpus is relevant. This finding is conditional on source relevance and recoverable citations. The presence of a citation alone does not establish correctness.

3.1 Main finding

Evidence should be evaluated in the context of the original research question. A passage may be topically similar while failing to support the specific claim being made.

Readers should inspect the original excerpt rather than treating a generated reference as a guarantee of reliability.

3.2 Conditions and limits

A fluent answer may still contain unsupported details. Explicit boundaries help the reader decide when the finding can be reused in another setting.

Preserving those conditions makes a research note easier to verify and keeps future writing grounded in its sources.

Research fixture for the reading workflow. Original excerpts remain available beside the conversation.
